# Feature review, 8 Oct 2026

buylog's job is a short loop: record a price, compare it in one currency, remember what you want, record what you bought, and see what you spent. Quotes, price alerts, purchase orders, and imports convert through `ForexService`. A watchlist target does not.

By service-class size, 2,491 of 5,599 lines in `services.py` (44%) are that loop. 2,803 lines (50%) sit beside it. Another 305 lines are `WatchlistService` and `PurchaseListService`, a second copy of behavior the loop already has. Quote status and `Product.category` live inside classes counted with the loop, so 305 undercounts the overlap.

Four ideas have more than one record: the price threshold, the purchase, the want-list, and product classification. The tasks are in `TODO.md`.

## How this was counted

`services.py` has 29 service classes. A class's size is the line span from its declaration to the next class. The file's service bodies add up to 5,599 lines.

`ReportService` is the largest class, at 640 lines. `QuoteService` is next, at 430. The TUI is a second interface, about 2,560 lines. The CLI has 35 top-level commands. The schema has 19 tables.

The commands that are the loop: `add`, `list`, `compare`, `history`, `alert`, `po`, `spend`, `fx`, `check`, `backup`, `migrate`.

## Essential

Without these, the sentence in the README fails.

- **Catalog.** Brand, product, vendor. Currency on the vendor is the field quotes need.

- **Quote.** One observed price, with discount, shipping, tax, and the currency that was typed. Stored in USD. The original amount and currency are kept and shown.

- **Forex.** Dated rates, refreshed from Frankfurter, so quotes in different currencies compare. Unique on `(code, date)`.

- **Comparison.** Best price by product, search, category, or brand, on total cost.

- **Price history.** The record that a quote's price changed. Trends, sparklines, and scrape refresh read it.

- **Price alert.** One threshold, with a currency. `buylog check` evaluates it against the current best price, converting the threshold first.

- **Purchase order.** The committed buy: quantity, dates, invoice, native total, and `grand_total_usd`.

- **Spend.** By month, vendor, and category, and against the best quote on file. Cancelled orders are excluded. Undated orders count on `created_at`.

- **Backup and migration.** `~/.buylog/buylog.db` can take a schema change and be restored.

## One purchase, two records

`buylog spend`, the variance report, and `grand_total_usd` read purchase orders. `QuoteService.set_status` writes `ordered` or `received` on the quote and commits. It does not create an order. A quote marked received never appears in spend.

The words overlap. Quote status is `considering`, `ordered`, `received`. A purchase order is `pending`, `approved`, `ordered`, `shipped`, `received`, or `cancelled`, and it carries quantity and dates. The order is the record spend can add up. The quote status is the one the TUI shows on every quote row, and the one `buylog status` writes.

High in `TODO.md`: one record for a purchase, and spend has to see it.

## Same idea, more than one record

**Price threshold.** `PriceAlert` stores a threshold and a currency. `PriceAlertService.check_alerts` converts before comparing. `Watchlist.target_price` is a bare float. `MonitorService.check` treats a target as met when `best.value <= entry.target_price`, and `best.value` is the USD-normalized quote. A target typed as euros is compared to dollars. Alerts and watchlist targets are also two commands, two tables, and two TUI tabs for one check.

High in `TODO.md`: one threshold, converted the way alerts already are.

**Want.** Three signals mean you might buy something.

- A purchase list is a named set of specific quotes. That grain is the useful one. `total_value` sums `total_cost`.

- A watchlist entry is a product. With a target price it is the alert above. Without one it is only a flag.

- Quote status `considering` is a flag on a single quote.

Medium in `TODO.md`: decide which of these stays, and derive the others from it.

**Classification.** `Product.category` is one string. Tags are many labels on any entity, and the color column is unused in the terminal. Specifications are a typed attribute schema: text, number, boolean, unit, required, min, max. `compare --category` and spend-by-category read the string. A tag or a filled-in spec does not change either report.

Medium in `TODO.md`: the classification the reports read should be the one a person can file a product under.

`Vendor.url` and `Vendor.website` are the small version of the same problem. Low in `TODO.md`.

## Beside the loop

These hang off quotes, products, and orders. The loop still closes if they go. That is the right shape for a side feature. The weight is uneven: the HTML report renderer is larger than the quote service, and the TUI repeats the colliding want-states as separate tabs (Alerts, Lists, Watchlist).

- Vendor contact fields: address, tax id, payment terms, phone, email. Separate from `url` / `website`.

- Specifications. Three services, 453 lines.

- Notes and tags, 323 lines. Notes also exist as columns on watchlist rows and purchase orders.

- Import and export, 423 lines. xlsx, csv, and yaml/json templates: three parsers for the same tables.

- HTML reports, 640 lines. Price comparison, purchase summary, vendor analysis, spend. The CLI already prints these figures.

- Scraping, 393 lines. Read a page, store `source_url`, refresh later, append price history.

- Notifications. stdout, ntfy, webhook, desktop, email. They deliver what `check` already decided.

- Receipts, clipboard, and opening a vendor URL.

- Duplicate-name merge for vendors and products.

- Audit log. The row has `user` and `ip_address`, columns a local single-user CLI does not fill. In 0.2.0 the table was missing at startup and a failed audit write poisoned the session. That startup bug is fixed. The columns are still the web-app shape.

- The TUI, about 2,560 lines, ten tabs.

CLI, TUI, HTML, and Excel are four views of one model. One of them is enough to run the ledger.
